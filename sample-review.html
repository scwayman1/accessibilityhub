WEEK 3 / SYNTHETIC COURSE HANDOUT

Observe, Describe, Discuss

Learning goal: describe one observation in clear, specific language.

Before class: read the short scenario and note one question.

During class: discuss your observation with a partner.

After class: revise your note so another reader can follow it.

This sample intentionally leaves out title and primary-language metadata so the review has clear, limited examples to discuss.

3 · Improve — Fix Lab

Two supported details, fixed on a copy.

In the rehearsal behind this sample, the Fix Lab added a document title and a primary language to a copy of the handout. Structure, meaning, and everything that needs educator judgment stayed exactly where it belongs—with the educator.

4 · Verify — what changed

Exactly two signals changed. The original file did not.

On the rechecked copy, Document title and Primary language moved from “Needs attention” to “Verified signal.” Every other signal stayed the same, and the structure question still waits for its author.

Each applied change is logged in an evidence receipt—what changed, when, and the before/after file fingerprints.

Signals and judgment work together

Useful review does not pretend every question has the same kind of answer.

A focused review can point to document details that are present or absent. Educators still bring the context needed for images, reading order, tables, links, and whether course material makes sense for the people using it.

  • “Needs attention” means there is a concrete detail to address—not an overall grade.
  • “Review recommended” keeps meaning and context visible for the people who own them.
  • “Verified signal” is one specific piece of machine evidence—never combined into an overall result.
  • “Not assessed” names what this review did not look at, instead of hiding it.
  • Supported fixes are applied to a copy and checked again; the original stays unchanged.
  • This sample offers a practical walkthrough, not a conformance, legal, or accessibility determination.
