Sample review: Week 3 course handout

The paper view and review panel below are a fixed illustration of one review—not a live document workspace.

WEEK 3 / SAMPLE COURSE HANDOUT

Observe, Describe, Discuss

Learning goal: describe one observation in clear, specific language.

Before class: read the short scenario and note one question.

During class: discuss your observation with a partner.

After class: revise your note so another reader can follow it.

This sample intentionally leaves out title and primary-language details so the review has clear, limited examples to discuss.

Every detail lands in one of four lanes. Nothing is combined into an overall grade.

!Needs attention

Add a document title

No title was recorded inside the PDF, so screen readers and browser tabs announce the file name instead of the handout’s name.

!Needs attention

Declare a primary language

The PDF does not say what language it is written in, so assistive technology has to guess how to pronounce the text.

?Review recommended

Tags and reading order

The sample has no marked structure. Whether headings and reading order carry the right meaning needs a person who knows the material.

✓Verified signal

Text can be read as text

The words on the page are real, extractable text—not a picture of text—so screen readers and copy‑paste can use them.

✓Verified signal

The single page of this handout was readable by the reviewer with no damage or encryption in the way.

Review completeness — 4 areas not assessed

This review did not look at color contrast, table structure, form fields, or meaning carried by color alone. Nothing in this report speaks to those either way—they are simply outside what this review checks, and they stay named here rather than hidden.

3 · Improve — Fix Lab

Two supported details, fixed on a copy.

In this run, the Fix Lab added a document title (“Observe, Describe, Discuss”) and a primary language (English) to a copy of the handout. Structure, meaning, and everything that needs educator judgment stayed exactly where it belongs—with the educator.

4 · Check again

Exactly two signals changed. The original file did not.

The reviewer ran again on the updated copy. Here is the whole difference:

  • !Needs attention→✓Verified signalDocument title — a title is now present in the copy.
  • !Needs attention→✓Verified signalPrimary language — the copy now declares English.

Every other signal stayed the same, the structure question still waits for its author, and the original handout is untouched. Each applied change is logged in an evidence receipt—what changed, when, and the before/after file fingerprints.

Signals and judgment work together

Useful review does not pretend every question has the same kind of answer.

A focused review can point to document details that are present or absent. Educators still bring the context needed for images, reading order, tables, links, and whether course material makes sense for the people using it.

  • “Needs attention” means there is a concrete detail to address—not an overall grade.
  • “Review recommended” keeps meaning and context with the people who own them.
  • “Verified signal” is one specific piece of machine evidence—never combined into an overall pass.
  • “Not assessed” names what this review did not look at, instead of hiding it.
  • Supported fixes are applied to a copy and checked again; the original stays unchanged.
  • This sample is a practical walkthrough, not a conformance, legal, or certification determination.
